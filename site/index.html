While you're away

A team of buddies
that works without you.

Each buddy owns one job, with its own chat, memory, model and tools. They run on a schedule, hand their results to each other, and only interrupt you when something needs your OK.

BrieferChecks your calendar at 9:00 every morning
→
WriterGets the brief and starts on its own
→
YouRead one answer, approve what matters

Hand off the answer

When one buddy finishes its job, the buddies you picked get the answer and start on their own. A buddy with several inputs waits for all of them.

Ask the team

A buddy can ask up to four teammates at once, split work into side threads, or draft a new teammate. Making one always needs your OK.

Threads side by side

Every chat with a buddy runs on its own. Send a message mid-job and it can steer the work in progress instead of waiting.

Backup models

If a model hits a limit or goes down before answering, the next one takes over. A backup that leaves your Mac is flagged as such.

A session of its own

Run a buddy on your Claude subscription with a kept Claude Code session per conversation, started fresh when it fills up.

Tidies its own notes

Every 20 messages a buddy reviews what it remembered and fixes it. Notes you wrote are never touched.

Write

Drafts it. You send it.

“Draft an email to Sam about moving Friday's meeting.” A finished draft opens with Sam's address from Contacts, and you can edit every word. “Text mom I'm running late” finds the right person and shows the message before Messages sends it.

  • Nothing leaves your Mac until you press Send
  • Riskier actions show a card you can edit
Browse

Runs the errand in your real browser

“Open Wikipedia and search for the Enigma machine.” Wisp works through the page itself, in its own tab (never the one you are reading), then tells you where it landed. “Organize my tabs” groups Chrome by site or topic and closes duplicates.

  • Password and payment fields are refused outright
  • Irreversible clicks always ask first
Arrange

Tidy your desk by voice

“Put Dictionary on the left and TextEdit on the right.” Halves, quarters, thirds, maximize, center and next display, with no model call and a one-line confirmation in the notch.

Guide

Walks you through it, then does it

Ask how to do something and Wisp turns the answer into steps over your live screen. It finds each next control as the screen changes, ticks steps off as you click, and can click for you.

  • “Do it for me” never touches anything irreversible
  • Works in any app, on any monitor
Teach

How it works

Hold. Speak. Done.

  1. 1

    Hold ⌃⌥ and talk

    From any app. Tap instead to type, or double-tap ⌃ for hands-free. Your words are transcribed on the Neural Engine, in 25 languages.

  2. 2

    Wisp looks and decides

    Rigid requests run directly. Everything else goes to your model with a small screenshot, the Accessibility tree and on-screen text.

  3. 3

    It acts, and asks first

    Answers appear in the notch, drafts open, buddies and tools get to work. Anything irreversible waits for your OK.

Before you try it

Questions, answered plainly.

Is it really free?

Yes. Wisp is open source under the MIT license, and the default setup costs nothing to run because the model, speech and voice are all on your Mac. If you choose a cloud model, you pay that provider with your own key or subscription.

What leaves my Mac?

By default, nothing. Speech to text, voice, OCR and the local model all run on-device. The only network use by default is a one-time download of the speech models and optional weather and places lookups, which send a place name or query, never a screenshot. If you pick a cloud model, your transcript and screenshots go to that provider, and Wisp says so.

Can it do something I didn't want?

Anything that sends, deletes, pays, posts, invites or runs a command shows a card first, even if you turned on auto-approve, and you can edit the wording before you allow it. Password and payment fields in the browser are refused outright. Scheduled automations only get tools that never prompt.

Which Macs does it need?

An Apple-silicon Mac on macOS 26 or later, since the notch uses Liquid Glass. 16 GB of memory is recommended for the local model. You need Xcode 26 to build it.

Does it work in my apps?

Pointing, walkthroughs and screen reading work anywhere: native apps through Accessibility and everything else through on-device OCR. Browser control works with Chrome and Safari.

Why isn't there a download button?

There is no notarized build published yet, and I'd rather say so than point you at something half-finished. Building it takes a few minutes in Xcode, and signed releases are on the way.
